# Process
## Which tools I used, and for what

I used Codex with GPT-6 Astra to turn the video into reading notes, compare essay topics, organise my ideas, and check the word count. In practice, I found that humans are often emotional and easily carried away, while AI remains consistently rational and level-headed; when I became too absorbed in a particular idea and drifted off topic, it would often help redirect me back to the original line of reasoning. 

Initially, I wanted to choose almost every topic because each connected to my experience. I shared my background and arguments with GPT and treated our conversation as a “group discussion”. Together, we narrowed the focus and developed a clearer structure. I supplied the development-time estimates, thoughts on professional terminology, and the analogy about hiring aerospace engineers to fix “Hello world” code. My experience with Unreal Blueprints, MCP AI, and GPT for analysing game design documents provided the practical background.

## One thing it produced that I kept, and why it was good

I kept the suggested structure: “reading versus writing”, supported by “the gap between describing and specifying”. It connected my experience to the assignment and helped explain why I still want to learn programming, without drifting into general predictions about AI replacing developers.

## One thing it produced that I rejected, and why it was wrong, generic, or not what I think

The AI suggested replacing my percentages with a general statement: initial setup was quick, but refinement took most of my time. Although this avoided implying statistical precision, it weakened how I wanted to describe my experience.

I kept the 5%/95% split and hypothetical 150–200% comparison, while clarifying that they were estimates rather than measurements. I accepted the caution about evidence, but not the suggestion to remove the figures.

I also rejected a hypothetical conflict between an AI-generated mechanic and level design. It illustrated the argument, but was not my experience. I replaced it with the actual trap bug from my project. A plausible example is not automatically valid evidence for a personal reflection.

Sometimes, the AI offered too many ideas that sounded “correct”, like arguments collected from the internet. I needed to keep the essay grounded in my experience, decide which suggestions expressed my views, and acknowledge the AI’s contribution. The collaboration depended on how specific I was and how carefully I evaluated its responses.
