# Process
## Which tools I used, and for what

For this assignment, I used Codex and GPT-6 Astra model. They helped turn Dylan Beattie’s video into reading notes, compare possible essay topics, organise my ideas, draft the essay and also help me count the words :) .

I gave the AI my background, real experiences; such as estimates of the time I spent, and my thoughts on 'terms'. I also supplied the analogy about hiring aerospace engineers to fix “Hello world” code. My use of Unreal Blueprints with MCP AI, and GPT to analyse game design documents, provided the practical background for the essay. The AI’s main roles were helping me brainstorm at the beginning and improving the structure of the writing.

## One thing it produced that I kept, and why it was good.

I kept the structure the AI suggested after discussing my background and views: focusing on “reading versus writing”, supported by “the gap between describing and specifying”. This connected my experience to the assignment and helped me explain why I still want to learn programming, without turning the essay into a general prediction about whether AI will replace developers.

## One thing it produced that I rejected, and why it was wrong, generic, or not what I think.

The AI suggested simplifying my time estimates into a general statement: AI completed most of the initial setup quickly, while the refinements took most of my time. I specifically asked to keep the original percentages. The simpler version made the figures less likely to be mistaken for precise statistics, but it also lost something important about how I wanted to describe my experience.

The final essay therefore keeps the 5% and 95% time split, along with the hypothetical 150–200% comparison. It also makes clear that these are estimates, not measured results. I accepted the point about being careful with evidence, but did not use the suggestion to remove the percentages.

The AI also proposed a hypothetical example involving a conflict between an AI-generated gameplay mechanic and the level design. I did not use it. It helped illustrate a possible argument, but it was not something I had experienced. I replaced it with the actual trap bug from my project. An example can sound plausible without being valid evidence for a personal reflection.

I sometimes felt that the AI was throwing too many ideas at me that sounded “correct”, like a collection of arguments gathered from the internet. My responsibility was to keep the essay grounded in my own experience, decide which suggestions actually expressed my views, and be honest about the AI’s contribution. How useful this collaboration was depended on how specific I could be and how carefully I considered its responses.
